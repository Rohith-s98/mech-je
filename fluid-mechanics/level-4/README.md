## How to use Level 4

Take these mocks only after finishing Levels 1 to 3 on all 10 topics. Each mock mixes every topic, the way the real paper does.

- **On the website:** press Start on a mock. It works like the exam's CBT screen: 25 questions, 25 minutes, one question at a time, a question palette and Mark for review. When the time runs out, the test submits itself.
- **Marking:** +1 for a right answer, −1/3 for a wrong one, 0 for a skip. That is the penalty in RRB JE, Kerala PSC and SSC JE Paper 2.
- **When to guess:** a blind guess among four options gains nothing on average (1/4 × 1 − 3/4 × 1/3 = 0). Rule out one option and a guess among the other three is worth +1/9 of a mark. Rule out two and it is worth +1/3. So guess only after you have eliminated at least one option.
- **After the test:** the review shows every question with your answer, the right one and the full solution. Read why each wrong option is wrong, not just the right one. Weak topics link back to the same section in Levels 1 to 3.
- **Reading here instead:** the solution sits right under each question, so cover it before you answer.
- **Retakes:** wait about a week. An immediate retake mostly tests your memory of the answers.

Each question names its exam, date and topic number, and Topic N is section N in Levels 1 to 3. In test mode the exam and topic are hidden, because spotting which idea a question tests is part of the skill. None of these questions appear in Levels 1 to 3. Each mock follows the weighting below.

About two thirds of the Fluid Mechanics questions test one fact: a property and its unit, what an instrument measures, which dimensionless number is which force ratio, or which turbine suits which head. The rest are short numericals: a pressure from a depth, a capillary rise, a jet force, a head loss ratio or a specific speed. In the mock you have one minute per question, so the aim is to recall the fact or formula at once and keep the arithmetic short. ## Speed toolkit

Most Fluid Mechanics questions in RRB JE, SSC JE and Kerala PSC need one formula or one fact. Learn the values below by heart, and a two-minute question takes 30 seconds.

### Ready values

| Quantity | Value to remember |
| --- | --- |
| Water and mercury | Water: ρ = 1000 kg/m³, γ = 9.81 kN/m³. Mercury: specific gravity 13.6. |
| Atmosphere | 1 atm = 101.3 kPa = 10.3 m of water = 760 mm of mercury |
| Viscosity units | 1 poise = 0.1 N·s/m². 1 stoke = 1 cm²/s = 10⁻⁴ m²/s. Water at 20°C: about 1 centipoise. |
| Capillary rise of water | About 30 mm in a clean 1 mm glass tube (σ ≈ 0.073 N/m, θ = 0) |
| Orifice coefficients | Sharp-edged orifice: Cc 0.61 to 0.64, Cv 0.97 to 0.99, Cd about 0.62 |
| Meter coefficients | Venturimeter Cd 0.95 to 0.99. Orifice meter Cd about 0.6 to 0.65. |
| Mouthpieces | External, running full: Cd about 0.86. Convergent-divergent: Cd about 1. Borda's (internal) running free: Cc = 0.5, Cd = 0.5. Running full: Cd = 0.707. |
| Pipe flow regimes | Laminar below Re 2000, turbulent above 4000, transition in between. Some books put the limit at 2300. |
| Laminar pipe flow | f = 64/Re (Darcy). V(max) = 2 × V(mean). Energy correction factor α = 2, momentum correction factor β = 4/3. |
| Minor loss coefficients | Sharp entry 0.5. Exit into a tank 1.0. Sudden expansion: (V₁ − V₂)²/2g. |
| Dimensionless numbers | Reynolds: inertia/viscous. Froude: √(inertia/gravity). Euler: √(inertia/pressure). Weber: √(inertia/surface tension). Mach: √(inertia/elastic). |
| Turbine types | Pelton: impulse, tangential flow, high head. Francis: reaction, radial or mixed flow, medium head. Kaplan: reaction, axial flow, low head, adjustable blades. |
| Turbine specific speed (metric, P in kW) | Pelton, one jet: 8.5 to 30. Pelton, more jets: 30 to 51. Francis: 51 to 255. Kaplan and propeller: 255 to 860. |
| Speed ratio (u/√(2gH)) | Pelton 0.43 to 0.47. Francis 0.6 to 0.9. Kaplan 1.4 to 2.0. |
| Runaway speed | Pelton 1.8 to 1.9 times normal speed. Francis 2 to 2.2 times. Kaplan 2.5 to 3 times. |
| Pumps | Centrifugal: needs priming, low head and high discharge. Reciprocating: self-priming, high head and low discharge. Pumps in series add head; pumps in parallel add discharge. |

### Relations that save a step

| Relation | Use it when |
| --- | --- |
| p = ρgh = 9.81 × s × h kPa | Pressure at depth h (m) in a liquid of specific gravity s. Pressures from layers add. |
| p(abs) = p(atm) + p(gauge), p(vac) = p(atm) − p(abs) | Converting between absolute, gauge and vacuum pressure |
| h = 4σ cos θ/(ρgd) | Capillary rise. It goes as 1/d, so a tube half as wide gives twice the rise. |
| F = ρgAȳ, h\* = ȳ + I(G)/(Aȳ) | Force on a submerged plane and its centre of pressure. A vertical rectangle with its top at the surface: h\* = 2h/3. A vertical circle touching the surface: h\* = 5d/8. |
| GM = I/V − BG, T = 2πk/√(g · GM) | Metacentric height of a floating body, and its period of rolling |
| p/ρg + V²/2g + z = constant | Bernoulli along a streamline, steady, ideal and incompressible. V = √(2gh) for an orifice jet or a Pitot tube. |
| Cd = Cc × Cv | Any orifice or mouthpiece |
| Q = (2/3)Cd L√(2g) H^(3/2), Q = (8/15)Cd√(2g) tan(θ/2) H^(5/2) | Rectangular notch, then V-notch. A 90° V-notch with Cd 0.6 gives Q = 1.417 H^(5/2). |
| hf = fLV²/(2gd) = fLQ²/(12.1d⁵) | Darcy-Weisbach. At the same discharge, hf goes as L/d⁵. An equivalent pipe has L/d⁵ = sum of Lᵢ/dᵢ⁵. |
| t(c) ≤ 2L/c is rapid closure | Water hammer. The pressure rise on rapid closure is ρcV. |
| δ\* = δ/2, θ = δ/6 | Displacement and momentum thickness for the linear profile u/U = y/δ |
| F = ρaV² | Jet striking a fixed flat plate normally. On a fixed inclined plate the normal force is ρaV² sin θ. On a moving plate, use (V − u) in place of V. |
| N(s) = N√P/H^(5/4), N(s) = N√Q/H^(3/4) | Specific speed of a turbine (P in kW), then of a pump |
| u = −∂ψ/∂y, v = ∂ψ/∂x, u = −∂φ/∂x | Stream and potential functions, with the sign convention used in Bansal and in the SSC JE keys. Other books flip the signs; the speeds come out the same. |

### Unit traps

- **Poise and stoke.** 1 poise is 0.1 N·s/m², not 1. 1 stoke is 10⁻⁴ m²/s.
- **Kilogram-force.** 1 kgf/cm² is about 98.1 kPa, or 10 m of water. In a liquid of specific gravity s, the same pressure is a head of 10/s m.
- **Mercury columns.** 1 m of mercury is 13.6 m of water.
- **Gauge or absolute.** Read which one the question asks before you add the atmosphere.
- **Specific weight.** It is in N/m³ or kN/m³, never in kg/m³.
- **Specific speed units.** The turbine ranges above use P in kW and H in m. With P in metric horsepower the numbers come out about 1.17 times larger.
- **Diameter or radius.** The capillary formula above uses the diameter.

### Eliminating options

- **Check the order of magnitude.** A jet of 0.01 m² at 20 m/s carries ρaV² = 4000 N. An option of 40 N or 400 kN is out at once.
- **Check the direction.** Head loss, Cd below 1, gauge pressure below absolute: an option that breaks the direction is wrong.
- **Use the turbine ladder.** Head falls and discharge and specific speed rise in the order Pelton, Francis, Kaplan.
- **Read the exact word.** Gauge or vacuum, streamline or streak line, dynamic or kinematic viscosity: each pair has a different answer.
- **Statement and "NOT" questions.** Mark each statement true or false before you look at the options.

## Mock 1

25 questions, 25 minutes, +1 for a right answer and −1/3 for a wrong one. 5 questions come from Kerala PSC papers (2023, 2025 and 2026), 3 from RRB JE (2019) and 17 from other exams.

**Q1. Kerala PSC AE (Mechanical), 18 August 2025 · Topic 1.** For a Newtonian fluid: (a) Shear stress is proportional to shear strain (b) Rate of shear stress is proportional to shear strain (c) Shear stress is proportional to rate of shear strain (d) Rate of shear stress is proportional to rate of shear strain

*Answer: (c) Shear stress is proportional to rate of shear strain.*

Newton's law of viscosity is τ = μ du/dy, and du/dy is the rate of shear strain (the rate of angular deformation). A Newtonian fluid follows it with a constant μ, so a plot of τ against du/dy is a straight line through the origin.

- **(a):** Hooke's law for a solid in shear, τ = Gγ. A fluid at rest takes no shear stress however much it has been deformed.
- **(b) and (d):** a rate of change of stress does not appear in Newton's law at all.

**The trap:** (a) reads like the textbook line with one word missing. The word "rate" is what separates a fluid from a solid.

**Q2. RRB JE CBT 2 (Mechanical), 29 August 2019 · Topic 5.** Which of the following forces act(s) in a moving fluid? (a) Inertia force (b) Viscous force (c) Gravity force (d) All of the options

*Answer: (d) All of the options.*

A moving real fluid carries inertia, viscous, gravity and pressure forces, and where they matter, surface tension and compressibility forces too. The equations of motion are named by which forces they keep: Euler keeps gravity and pressure only, and Navier-Stokes adds viscosity.

- **(a), (b) and (c):** each acts, but none acts alone.

**Memory aid:** every dimensionless number compares inertia with one of the other forces: Reynolds with viscous, Froude with gravity, Euler with pressure, Weber with surface tension and Mach with elastic.

**Q3. SSC JE Mechanical, 7 June 2024 (Shift 2) · Topic 2.** The pressure, which is measured with reference to absolute vacuum pressure is called: (a) absolute pressure (b) gauge pressure (c) vacuum pressure (d) atmospheric pressure

*Answer: (a) Absolute pressure.*

Absolute pressure is measured from absolute zero, a perfect vacuum, so it is never negative. Gauge pressure is measured from the local atmosphere, so p(abs) = p(atm) + p(gauge).

- **(b) Gauge pressure:** its zero is the local atmospheric pressure.
- **(c) Vacuum pressure:** how far a pressure falls below atmospheric, p(vac) = p(atm) − p(abs).
- **(d) Atmospheric pressure:** one particular absolute pressure, about 101.3 kPa at sea level. It is not a way of measuring.

**The trap:** the word "vacuum" in the stem pulls you to (c). Vacuum pressure is counted downward from the atmosphere, not upward from a perfect vacuum.

**Q4. Kerala PSC AE (Mechanical), 18 August 2025 · Topic 9.** Which one of the following statements is TRUE? (a) Both Pelton and Francis turbines are impulse turbines (b) Francis turbine is a reaction turbine but Kaplan turbine is an impulse turbine (c) Francis turbine is an axial flow reaction turbine (d) Kaplan turbine is an axial flow reaction turbine

*Answer: (d) Kaplan turbine is an axial flow reaction turbine.*

In a Kaplan turbine the water enters and leaves the runner parallel to the shaft, so it is axial flow. Part of the head is still pressure when the water reaches the runner, so it is a reaction turbine.

- **(a):** Francis is a reaction turbine. Of the three, only Pelton is impulse.
- **(b):** Kaplan is reaction, like Francis.
- **(c):** the modern Francis turbine is mixed flow: water enters radially and leaves axially. It is not an axial flow machine.

**Memory aid:** Pelton is impulse and tangential flow, Francis is reaction and radial or mixed flow, and Kaplan is reaction and axial flow.

**Q5. SSC JE Mechanical, 9 October 2023 (Shift 2) · Topic 7.** Which of the following equations is used to calculate major losses in pipes? (a) Continuity equation (b) Momentum equation (c) Darcy-Weisbach equation (d) Reynolds's equation

*Answer: (c) Darcy-Weisbach equation.*

The major loss is the friction loss along the length of the pipe, hf = fLV²/(2gd), where f is the Darcy friction factor. Chezy's formula does the same job with a different constant.

- **(a) Continuity equation:** conservation of mass. It gives velocities from areas, not losses.
- **(b) Momentum equation:** it gives forces on bends and nozzles. The sudden expansion loss comes from it, but that is a minor loss.
- **(d) Reynolds's equation:** the Reynolds number tells you whether the flow is laminar or turbulent. It does not give the loss.

**Memory aid:** major loss is friction over the length; minor losses come from fittings, bends, entry, exit and sudden changes of area.

**Q6. Kerala PSC Deputy Engineer / AE (Mechanical), 18 June 2023 · Topic 3.** For a completely submerged body with centre of gravity 'G' and centre of buoyancy 'B', the condition of stability will be (a) G is located above B (b) G is located below B (c) G and B are coincident (d) None of the above

*Answer: (b) G is located below B.*

A fully submerged body has no water plane, so it has no metacentre above B. When it tilts, the weight acts through G and the buoyancy through B. The couple they make rights the body only if G is below B.

- **(a) G above B:** the couple turns the body further, so it is unstable.
- **(c) G and B coincident:** no couple at all, so the body is in neutral equilibrium.
- **(d):** (b) is correct.

**The trap:** a floating ship can be stable with G above B, because its metacentre rises above G. A submarine or a balloon has no such help, so G must sit below B.

**Q7. SSC JE Civil, 5 June 2024 (Shift 1) · Topic 10.** The priming of a centrifugal pump is necessary: (a) to remove air from the parts of the pump (b) to reduce pressure (c) to reduce the temperature of water (d) to increase discharge

*Answer: (a) To remove air from the parts of the pump.*

Priming fills the suction pipe, the casing and the delivery pipe up to the valve with water and drives the air out. The impeller makes the same head in metres of whatever fluid it holds. With air, about 800 times lighter than water, the pressure rise ρgH is far too small to lift water up the suction pipe.

- **(b):** a pump raises pressure. Priming does not lower it.
- **(c):** temperature has nothing to do with priming.
- **(d):** priming only makes pumping possible. The discharge depends on the impeller once it runs full of water.

**Memory aid:** a centrifugal pump must be primed; a reciprocating pump is self-priming.

**Q8. SSC JE Mechanical, 4 December 2025 (Shift 2) · Topic 4.** At point P(2,3), what is the magnitude of the velocity vector for the 2D flow with stream function ψ = 2xy? (a) 6 (b) 7.2 (c) 5 (d) 4

*Answer: (b) 7.2.*

u = −∂ψ/∂y = −2x = −4 and v = ∂ψ/∂x = 2y = 6. So V = √(4² + 6²) = √52 ≈ 7.2.

- **(a) 6:** the y component alone.
- **(c) 5:** √(4² + 3²), which uses y = 3 in place of 2y = 6.
- **(d) 4:** the x component alone.

**The trap:** do not stop at one component. The sign convention does not matter here, because both components are squared.

**Q9. RRB JE CBT 2 (Mechanical), 29 August 2019 · Topic 6.** \_\_\_ is a small opening of any cross section on the side or bottom of the tank on which fluid is flowing. (a) Weir (b) Notch (c) Mouth piece (d) Orifice

*Answer: (d) Orifice.*

An orifice is a small opening of any shape, circular, square or rectangular, in the side or bottom of a tank, with the liquid surface above its top edge. It is used to measure discharge.

- **(a) Weir:** a notch on a large scale, built across a river or channel.
- **(b) Notch:** an opening in the side of a tank whose top edge is above the liquid surface, so the liquid flows over it with a free surface.
- **(c) Mouthpiece:** a short tube, two to three diameters long, fitted to an orifice.

**The trap:** a notch and an orifice are both openings in a tank wall. The orifice is fully under water; the notch is not.

**Q10. SSC JE Mechanical, 22 January 2018 (Morning) · Topic 1.** The capillary rise at 20°C in a clean glass tube of 1 mm diameter containing water is approximately (a) 15 mm (b) 50 mm (c) 20 mm (d) 30 mm

*Answer: (d) 30 mm.*

For water in clean glass, θ ≈ 0. h = 4σ cos θ/(ρgd) = 4 × 0.0736/(1000 × 9.81 × 0.001) = 0.030 m = 30 mm.

- **(a) 15 mm:** half the answer. It comes from 2σ/(ρgr) with the diameter put in place of the radius.
- **(b) 50 mm and (c) 20 mm:** no value of σ near 0.073 N/m gives these.

**Memory aid:** water in a 1 mm glass tube rises about 30 mm. The rise goes as 1/d, so a 0.5 mm tube gives about 60 mm.

**Q11. DSSSB JE Civil, 20 November 2019 (Shift 3) · Topic 8.** The square root of the ratio of inertial forces of a flowing fluid to gravitational force is called: (a) Euler's number (b) Weber's number (c) Froude's number (d) Mach's number

*Answer: (c) Froude's number.*

Fr = √(inertia force/gravity force) = V/√(gL). It governs flows with a free surface: open channels, spillways, and the waves a ship makes.

- **(a) Euler's number:** √(inertia/pressure force) = V/√(p/ρ). It matters in pipe flow under pressure.
- **(b) Weber's number:** √(inertia/surface tension force). It matters for droplets and capillary flow.
- **(d) Mach's number:** √(inertia/elastic force) = V/C. It matters in compressible flow.

**Memory aid:** in Bansal's definitions only Reynolds is a plain ratio of forces. Froude, Euler, Weber and Mach are square roots.

**Q12. BDL Management Trainee (Mechanical), 16 April 2022 · Topic 2.** If atmospheric pressure at a place is 750 mm Hg, then a gauge pressure of 150 mm Hg refers to (a) 800 mm Hg absolute (b) 700 mm Hg absolute (c) 600 mm Hg absolute (d) 900 mm Hg absolute

*Answer: (d) 900 mm Hg absolute.*

p(abs) = p(atm) + p(gauge) = 750 + 150 = 900 mm Hg.

- **(c) 600 mm Hg:** 750 − 150. That is the right working only for a vacuum of 150 mm Hg.
- **(a) 800 mm Hg and (b) 700 mm Hg:** neither the sum nor the difference.

**The trap:** subtract only for a vacuum reading. A gauge reading is added to the local atmosphere.

**Q13. MPPGCL JE Mechanical, 1 June 2024 · Topic 9.** What will be the force exerted by a jet of water against a stationary flat plate perpendicular to the axis of the jet if the diameter and the velocity of the jet are 4 cm and 25 m/s, respectively? (a) 25,000 N (b) 785 N (c) 31.4 N (d) 0 N

*Answer: (b) 785 N.*

a = (π/4) × 0.04² = 1.257 × 10⁻³ m². F = ρaV² = 1000 × 1.257 × 10⁻³ × 25² = 785 N.

- **(a) 25,000 N:** 1000 × 0.04 × 25², with the diameter put in place of the area.
- **(c) 31.4 N:** ρaV, which is the mass flow rate in kg/s, not a force.
- **(d) 0 N:** the leaving jet has no velocity normal to the plate, but the force comes from the momentum the jet loses, which is all of it.

**The trap:** ρaV is the mass flow rate. Multiply by V once more to get the force.

**Q14. Kerala PSC AE (Mechanical, Ports), 15 July 2026 · Topic 5.** Which of the following devices is not used for measuring rate of flow in pipeline? (a) Venturimeter (b) Orifice meter (c) Rotameter (d) Pitot tube

*Answer: (d) Pitot tube.*

A Pitot tube measures the velocity at one point, from the extra head V²/2g it picks up when it stops the flow at its tip. To get the flow rate from it you would need readings across the whole section.

- **(a) Venturimeter:** gives the discharge from the pressure drop between the inlet and the throat.
- **(b) Orifice meter:** the same principle with a thin plate. It is cheaper but loses more head.
- **(c) Rotameter:** a float rises in a tapered tube, and its height reads the flow rate directly.

**The trap:** all four work on Bernoulli's equation. Only the Pitot tube reads at a single point.

**Q15. SSC JE Mechanical, 11 October 2023 (Shift 2) · Topic 7.** The Energy Gradient line will\_\_\_\_\_\_. (a) lie above the Hydraulic Gradient line (b) lie below the Hydraulic Gradient line (c) coincide with pipe axis (d) coincide with Hydraulic Gradient line

*Answer: (a) Lie above the Hydraulic Gradient line.*

The hydraulic gradient line joins the values of p/ρg + z. The energy gradient line adds the velocity head V²/2g, which is always positive, so it sits above the HGL by V²/2g.

- **(b):** that would need a negative velocity head.
- **(c):** the pipe axis marks z alone. The HGL lies above it wherever the pressure is above atmospheric.
- **(d):** only where V = 0, as at the surface of a reservoir.

**Memory aid:** the energy line always falls in the direction of flow, because of losses. The hydraulic line can rise where the pipe widens and the velocity drops.

**Q16. SSC JE Mechanical, 9 October 2023 (Shift 2) · Topic 1.** The dimensional formula for specific gravity is given by: (a) M1L0T1 (b) M1L1T0 (c) M0L0T0 (d) M0L1T1

*Answer: (c) M0L0T0.*

Specific gravity is the density of the fluid divided by the density of water at 4°C. A ratio of two like quantities has no dimensions.

- **(a), (b) and (d):** each carries mass or length, so none can be a pure ratio.

**The trap:** specific weight is a different quantity, in N/m³ with dimensions ML⁻²T⁻². Specific volume is in m³/kg. Only specific gravity is a plain number.

**Q17. SSC JE Mechanical, 27 September 2019 (Morning) · Topic 3.** Determine the total pressure on a circular plate of diameter 1.5 m which is placed vertically in water in such a way that the centre of plate is 3 m below the surface of water (a) 52002.81 N (b) 520.028 N (c) 52.002 N (d) 5200.281 N

*Answer: (a) 52002.81 N.*

F = ρgAȳ with ȳ the depth of the centroid. A = (π/4) × 1.5² = 1.767 m², so F = 1000 × 9.81 × 1.767 × 3 = 52,002.81 N. Without rounding the area it is about 52,007 N.

- **(b), (c) and (d):** the same digits with the wrong power of ten.

**The trap:** use the depth of the centre of the plate, 3 m. The total force does not depend on whether the plate is vertical or inclined, only on the area and the depth of its centroid.

**Q18. RRB JE CBT 2 (Mechanical), 19 September 2019 (Shift 2) · Topic 10.** The specific speed of a centrifugal pump is defined as the speed of geometrically similar pump which would (a) Deliver unit discharge at unit power (b) Produce unit power with unit head (c) Deliver unit discharge at unit head (d) Require unit power to develop unit head

*Answer: (c) Deliver unit discharge at unit head.*

N(s) = N√Q/H^(3/4) for a pump. It is the speed of a similar pump that delivers 1 m³/s against a head of 1 m.

- **(a) and (d):** power is in a turbine's specific speed, not a pump's.
- **(b):** the turbine definition: a similar turbine that produces 1 kW under a head of 1 m.

**Memory aid:** each machine is rated by what it delivers. A pump delivers discharge, so its specific speed uses Q. A turbine delivers power, so its specific speed uses P.

**Q19. NBCC JE Civil, 10 June 2018 · Topic 2.** An open tank contains 1 m deep water with 50 cm depth of oil of specific gravity 0.8 above it. The intensity of pressure at the bottom of the tank is (a) 4 kN/m² (b) 10 kN/m² (c) 12 kN/m² (d) 14 kN/m²

*Answer: (d) 14 kN/m².*

The pressures from the two layers add. Oil: 0.8 × 9.81 × 0.5 = 3.92 kPa. Water: 9.81 × 1 = 9.81 kPa. The total is 13.73 kPa, or 14 kN/m² with g = 10.

- **(a) 4 kN/m²:** the oil layer alone.
- **(b) 10 kN/m²:** the water layer alone.
- **(c) 12 kN/m²:** 0.8 × 10 × 1.5, as if the whole 1.5 m were oil.

**The trap:** each layer adds its own ρgh. Never use one density for the whole depth.

**Q20. DSSSB JE & Section Officer (Civil), 29 June 2022 (Shift 2) · Topic 6.** The discharge equation for a triangular V-notch with coefficient of discharge 0.6 is given by \_\_\_\_\_\_\_. Where, Q = discharge through triangular V notch and H = head of water above V notch. (a) Q = 1.417H^(3/2) (b) Q = 1.417H^(2/3) (c) Q = 1.417H^(5/2) (d) Q = 1.417H^(1/2)

*Answer: (c) Q = 1.417H^(5/2).*

Q = (8/15) Cd √(2g) tan(θ/2) H^(5/2). For a right-angled notch tan 45° = 1, and with Cd = 0.6 the constant is (8/15) × 0.6 × 4.429 = 1.417.

- **(a) H^(3/2):** the power for a rectangular notch.
- **(b) H^(2/3):** the rectangular power turned upside down. No notch follows it.
- **(d) H^(1/2):** the power for an orifice.

**The trap:** the constant 1.417 holds only for a 90° notch with Cd = 0.6. The stem leaves the angle out because that is the standard case.

**Q21. Kerala PSC AE (Mechanical, Ports), 15 July 2026 · Topic 9.** In a Francis turbine, the value of speed ratio varies between (a) 0.10 to 0.15 (b) 0.15 to 0.30 (c) 0.6 to 0.9 (d) None of these

*Answer: (c) 0.6 to 0.9.*

The speed ratio is u₁/√(2gH), the runner's inlet blade speed against the jet speed the head could give. For a Francis turbine it lies between 0.6 and 0.9.

- **(a) 0.10 to 0.15:** too low for any turbine. Pelton, the slowest, runs at about 0.45.
- **(b) 0.15 to 0.30:** the flow ratio of a Francis turbine, V(f)/√(2gH).
- **(d):** (c) is the range.

**The trap:** (b) is a real Francis value, but for the flow ratio, not the speed ratio.

**Q22. UPRVUNL JE Mechanical, 24 October 2021 (Shift 2) · Topic 4.** In a flow, locus of particles which have earlier passed through a prescribed point is called \_\_\_\_\_\_. (a) Streakline (b) Timeline (c) Pathline (d) Streamline

*Answer: (a) Streakline.*

A streakline joins, at one instant, every particle that has passed through a fixed point. Dye fed steadily from a fixed nozzle draws one.

- **(b) Timeline:** a line of particles all marked at the same instant, then followed as they move.
- **(c) Pathline:** the track of one particle over time.
- **(d) Streamline:** a line tangent to the velocity at every point at one instant.

**Memory aid:** smoke from a chimney is a streakline. One tracer particle photographed with a long exposure draws a pathline.

**Q23. MPSC AE Civil Mains 2020 (Paper 2) · Topic 7.** A sudden closure of a valve at the downstream end of a pipe leading from a reservoir will cause a water hammer. The length of this pipe is 'L' and the speed of the pressure wave is 'a'. If the time of closure of valve is 'tc', then valve closure is said to be rapid only when: (a) L/(2a) ≥ tc (b) L/a ≥ tc (c) 2L/a = tc (d) 2L/a ≥ tc

*Answer: (d) 2L/a ≥ tc.*

The pressure wave runs up to the reservoir and back in 2L/a. If the valve shuts within that time, the relief wave from the reservoir arrives too late, and the full rise ρaV builds up at the valve. That is rapid closure.

**Q13. UPSSSC JE Mechanical, 19 December 2021 · Topic 9.** Which of the given statements is correct regarding the primary function of a draft tube? (a) Reduce the pressure at the end of tube (b) Minimize the loss of kinetic energy at the outlet (c) Allow cavitation (d) Allow back flow

*Answer: (b) Minimize the loss of kinetic energy at the outlet.*

The draft tube is a widening pipe from the runner exit down to the tail race. It slows the water, so less kinetic energy is thrown away at the exit. It also lets the turbine sit above the tail race without losing that height.

- **(a):** it lowers the pressure at its top, at the runner exit, not at its end. Its outlet is at tail race pressure.
- **(c):** cavitation is a danger the draft tube must be designed to avoid.
- **(d):** back flow is never wanted.

**Memory aid:** reaction turbines need a draft tube. A Pelton wheel has none, because it runs at atmospheric pressure.

**Q14. DSSSB JE Civil, 23 October 2019 (Shift 1) · Topic 5.** Venturimeter and pitot tube are used to measure: (a) Discharge and Velocity at a point, respectively (b) Velocity at a point and Pressure at a point, respectively (c) Pressure at a point and Discharge, respectively (d) Average velocity and Discharge, respectively

*Answer: (a) Discharge and velocity at a point, respectively.*

The venturimeter gives the discharge from the pressure drop between its inlet and throat. The Pitot tube gives the velocity at its tip, V = √(2gh), from the extra head it reads.

- **(b):** the venturimeter does not give a point velocity, and the Pitot tube's purpose is velocity, not pressure.
- **(c):** neither instrument is used to report a pressure.
- **(d):** the Pitot tube does not give the discharge.

**The trap:** "respectively" questions test the order as well as the pair. Check each instrument against its own position.

**Q15. RRB JE CBT 2 (Mechanical), 31 August 2019 · Topic 7.** If the Reynolds number is less than 2000, the flow in pipe is (a) Transitional from laminar to turbulent (b) Turbulent (c) Inviscid (d) Laminar

*Answer: (d) Laminar.*

Below a Reynolds number of 2000, viscous forces are strong enough to damp out disturbances, and the fluid moves in smooth layers.

- **(a) Transitional:** between 2000 and 4000.
- **(b) Turbulent:** above about 4000.
- **(c) Inviscid:** a fluid with no viscosity would have an infinite Reynolds number. No real flow is inviscid.

**Memory aid:** in laminar pipe flow f = 64/Re and the centre velocity is twice the mean.

**Q16. JSSC JE Re-exam (Paper I), 31 October 2022 · Topic 4.** Straight converging streamlines denote: (a) convective tangential acceleration (b) no acceleration (c) convective normal acceleration (d) both normal and tangential convective acceleration

*Answer: (a) Convective tangential acceleration.*

Converging streamlines mean the flow area shrinks along the flow, so the velocity rises along each streamline. That is tangential convective acceleration. The streamlines are straight, so there is no curvature and no normal acceleration.

- **(b):** the velocity changes along the line, so there is acceleration.
- **(c):** normal acceleration needs curved streamlines.
- **(d):** both together need streamlines that are curved and converging.

**Memory aid:** a change of speed along a line gives tangential acceleration; a change of direction, on a curve, gives normal acceleration.

**Q17. SSC JE Mechanical, 25 January 2018 (Evening) · Topic 10.** Multi-stage centrifugal pumps are used for (a) High discharge (b) High head and high discharge (c) High pressure (d) High efficiency

*Answer: (c) High pressure.*

A multistage pump has several impellers in series on one shaft. The same discharge passes through each, and the heads add, so it delivers a high head, that is a high pressure.

- **(a) High discharge:** pumps in parallel.
- **(b) High head and high discharge:** that would need stages in series and pumps in parallel together.
- **(d) High efficiency:** staging does not raise efficiency; each stage has its own losses.

**Memory aid:** boiler feed pumps and deep well pumps are multistage, because both must deliver a high head.

**Q18. MP Vyapam Sub Engineer (Civil), 4 September 2018 (Shift 2) · Topic 1.** Compressibility is the reciprocal of (a) Bulk modulus of elasticity (b) Rigidity Modulus of elasticity (c) Shear modulus of elasticity (d) Young's modulus of elasticity

*Answer: (a) Bulk modulus of elasticity.*

The bulk modulus is K = −dp/(dV/V), and compressibility is 1/K. For water K is about 2.1 GPa, so water is nearly incompressible.

- **(b) and (c):** rigidity modulus and shear modulus are the same thing, and a fluid at rest has none.
- **(d) Young's modulus:** for a solid stretched or compressed along one axis.

**Memory aid:** a high bulk modulus means hard to squeeze. Gases have a low K, liquids a high one.

**Q19. UPPSC AE Civil 2024 (Paper II), 28 September 2025 · Topic 6.** In Borda's mouthpiece, the coefficient of contraction is: (a) 0.50 (b) 0.58 (c) 0.707 (d) 0.60

*Answer: (a) 0.50.*

Borda's mouthpiece is a short tube projecting into the tank. Running free, a momentum balance shows the jet area is half the tube area, so Cc = 0.5, and with Cv ≈ 1, Cd = 0.5 too. Running full, Cc = 1 and Cd = 0.707.

- **(b) 0.58 and (d) 0.60:** close to orifice values, not Borda's mouthpiece.
- **(c) 0.707:** the coefficient of discharge when it runs full.

**The trap:** the stem does not say free or full. The coefficient of contraction quoted for Borda's mouthpiece is the running-free value, because running full it is simply 1.

**Q20. Kerala PSC AE (Mechanical, Ports), 15 July 2026 · Topic 9.** Constant head characteristics of Kaplan turbine for different gate openings shows (a) Unit discharge decrease with unit speed (b) Unit discharge increase with unit speed (c) Unit discharge does not vary with unit speed (d) Unit discharge first increase and then decrease with unit speed

*Answer: (b) Unit discharge increases with unit speed.*

The main, constant head characteristic curves plot unit discharge against unit speed for each gate opening. For a Kaplan turbine the unit discharge rises as the unit speed rises.

- **(a):** the Francis turbine, where unit discharge falls as unit speed rises.
- **(c):** the Pelton wheel, where the nozzle alone sets the discharge.
- **(d):** no standard turbine shows this.

**Memory aid:** on these curves Pelton is flat, Francis falls and Kaplan rises.

**Q21. AAI JE (Technical) 2018 · Topic 2.** A container contains water and oil of specific gravity 0.9. Oil is at the top with a depth of 1 m and water at the bottom with a height 2 m. Assuming g = 9.81 m/s², the pressure intensity at the bottom of the container in kPa will be: (a) 15.6 (b) 21.54 (c) 28.45 (d) 13.2

*Answer: (c) 28.45.*

Oil: 0.9 × 9.81 × 1 = 8.83 kPa. Water: 9.81 × 2 = 19.62 kPa. The total is 28.45 kPa.

- **(a), (b) and (d):** none equals the oil layer, the water layer or their sum.

**The trap:** a quick check settles it. The answer must lie between 3 m of oil (26.5 kPa) and 3 m of water (29.4 kPa), and only 28.45 does.

**Q22. DSSSB JE Civil, 20 November 2019 (Shift 1) · Topic 7.** Which of the following is true? (a) Hydraulic gradient line is constant in the direction of flow (b) Hydraulic gradient line always drops (c) Hydraulic gradient line always rises in the direction of flow (d) Hydraulic gradient line may or may not change in the direction of flow.

*Answer: (d) Hydraulic gradient line may or may not change in the direction of flow.*

The hydraulic gradient line joins the values of p/ρg + z. Friction makes it fall along a uniform pipe, but where the pipe widens, velocity head turns back into pressure and the line can rise. A pump lifts it too.

- **(a):** level only when there is no flow or no loss.
- **(b):** not at an enlargement or a pump.
- **(c):** it rises only at particular places.

**The trap:** (b) is true of the energy line, which always falls along the flow unless a pump adds energy. The hydraulic line need not fall.

**Q23. DFCCIL Executive (Mechanical), 28 September 2021 (Shift 1) · Topic 3.** Which of the following is/are correct about the metacentre and metacentric height? I) The metacentric height is affected by change in density. II) For a completely immersed body, the metacentric height is always zero. III) The metacentre always lies below the centre of gravity. (a) only II and III (b) I, II and III (c) only I and II (d) only I

*Answer: (d) Only I.*

I is true: in a denser liquid the body displaces less volume V, which changes BM = I/V and the position of B, and so GM. II is false: for a fully immersed body the metacentre is at B, so GM = BG, which is zero only if G and B coincide. III is false: in any stable floating body M lies above G.

- **(a), (b) and (c):** each includes II, which is false.

**Memory aid:** mark I true, II false, III false. Only (d) holds I alone.

**Q24. UKPSC JE Mechanical 2013 (Paper II) · Topic 5.** Bernoulli's equation can be derived from (a) Continuity equation (b) Newton's law of viscosity (c) Reynold transport theorem (d) Euler's equation

*Answer: (d) Euler's equation.*

Integrate Euler's equation along a streamline, dp/ρ + g dz + V dV = 0, with ρ constant, and you get p/ρ + gz + V²/2 = constant.

- **(a) Continuity equation:** conservation of mass. It carries no energy terms.
- **(b) Newton's law of viscosity:** Bernoulli assumes no viscosity at all.
- **(c) Reynolds transport theorem:** a general tool for writing conservation laws for a control volume. It leads to the momentum equation, but the standard step to Bernoulli is Euler's equation.

**The trap:** Euler's equation of motion and the Euler number are different things. Here it is the equation.

**Q25. ESE Civil 2016 (Paper 2) · Topic 8.** Statement (I): Reynolds number must be the same for model and the prototype when both are tested as immersed in a subsonic flow. Statement (II): A model should be geometrically similar to the prototype. (a) Both Statement (I) and Statement (II) are individually true and Statement (II) is the correct explanation of Statement (I) (b) Both Statement (I) and Statement (II) are individually true but Statement (II) is not the correct explanation of Statement (I) (c) Statement (I) is true but Statement (II) is false (d) Statement (I) is false but Statement (II) is true

*Answer: (b) Both are true, but (II) is not the correct explanation of (I).*

(I) is true: a body fully immersed in subsonic flow has no free surface and little compressibility, so viscous forces control it and Reynolds' model law applies. (II) is true: every model must be geometrically similar. But (II) holds whatever law applies, so it does not explain why Reynolds is the law here.

- **(a):** (II) does not explain (I).
- **(c) and (d):** both statements are true.

**The trap:** ask whether (II) gives the reason for (I) in particular. A truth that applies to every model explains nothing about one case.
